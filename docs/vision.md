# Guttr: Proposal and product vision

**Stage:** Exploratory concept, not a finished specification.

## Opportunity

Traditional digital forms are highly effective at collecting structured responses, but they give creators relatively limited tools for visual sequencing, narrative framing, and participatory comic-making.

Guttr proposes to make **comicification** a practical way of authoring a survey. Participants should be able to experience questions through sequences of panels, characters, images, and transitions while still benefiting from reliable survey interactions and data collection.

This concept emerges from Comic Survey Lab's work with comic-based surveys and creative workshops. Its potential effects on engagement, comprehension, or response quality remain empirical questions.

## Product proposition

A creative and research-oriented platform combining three useful interaction models:

- **Visual editing:** a canvas and reusable creative elements, analogous to familiar design tools.
- **Survey logic:** forms, answer inputs, branching, publishing, and response exports.
- **Versioning:** recognizable revisions, attribution, and histories for surveys and creative assets.

These analogies describe user experience ambitions, not integrations, feature-parity commitments, or implementation choices.

## Experiences to explore

### Main Builder

Compose surveys digitally using panels, bubbles, characters, environments, layout templates, answer inputs, and conditional logic.

### Experiment Room

Preserve a paper-first workflow: make a comic offline, bring it into the platform, identify panels and interaction areas, then turn it into a functioning survey. Image cleanup should be reversible if introduced.

### Respondent experience

The proposed respondent interface should keep narrative continuity while collecting structured answers. Text, voice, drawing, and short video are possible modalities, subject to privacy, accessibility, cost, and scope decisions.

### Community asset library

Artists could contribute or license collections of modular assets, such as consistent character expressions, poses, backgrounds, speech bubbles, and effects. Licensing and compensation models remain to be agreed.

## What the product should avoid

- Reducing comicification to clip art around conventional forms.
- A generic chatbot as the central creation workflow.
- Generating user-specific story scenes or images as a substitute for creative authorship.
- Treating published surveys as mutable without accounting for their research implications.
- Assuming research benefits before evaluating them.

## What is not yet decided

The MVP, exact survey feature parity, rights model, permissions, history semantics, hosting, multimodal limits, and research-export requirements are open. See [open decisions](open-decisions.md).
