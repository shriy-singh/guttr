# Research and evaluation agenda

**Status:** Questions and hypotheses, not demonstrated product outcomes.

## Motivating research questions

- Can comic-based survey design improve engagement compared with an equivalent conventional form?
- How does sequential visual framing affect question comprehension and interpretation?
- How do creative constraints, asset availability, and editor interaction affect the effort of authoring a comic survey?
- How does a paper-first workflow compare with direct digital composition for first-time comic creators?
- What do people experience as more accessible, expressive, or burdensome across text, voice, drawing, and video responses?
- How should publishing and asset revisions be preserved for research reproducibility?

## Possible evaluation dimensions

| Dimension | Illustrative measures |
| --- | --- |
| Authoring usability | Time to first functional survey, error rate, perceived workload |
| Respondent engagement | Completion, abandonment, optional qualitative feedback |
| Comprehension | Understanding checks or interview-based interpretation |
| Response quality | Missingness, relevance, depth, coding reliability |
| Accessibility | Keyboard and screen-reader usability, multimodal alternatives |
| Reproducibility | Ability to reconstruct the specific survey and asset versions |

These are candidate measures requiring an actual study protocol. No performance improvements are claimed.

## Workshop-informed starting point

A proposed facilitated workflow pairs survey authors with comic creators, asks teams to create a short comic survey, and allows peer review without showing reviewers the creators' identities. Details such as question count, review assignment, and consent should remain configurable rather than being treated as product defaults.

## Data and ethics

Before collecting real participant data, define consent, access rules, retention, deletion, anonymization or pseudonymization, and the treatment of potentially identifiable audio, drawing, or video responses.
