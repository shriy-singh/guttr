# Asset licensing, attribution, and artist participation

**This is a proposal for policy design, not a legal license or established compensation policy.**

Guttr may eventually include reusable character poses, expressions, backgrounds, speech bubbles, icons, stickers, textures, and animated elements.

## Principles for a trustworthy library

- Record the artist, source, asset version, and applicable license for every imported item.
- Distinguish the right to **use an asset in a published survey** from the right to **redistribute the editable asset** to other creators.
- Do not assume public availability means an image is licensed for scraping, remixing, commercial use, or redistribution.
- Keep software code licensing separate from artwork and third-party media licensing.
- Preserve attribution and revision history in project records.
- Define processes for takedowns, corrections, and license conflicts before launching a public library.

## Possible acquisition paths

1. Original assets commissioned for Guttr under explicit agreements.
2. Direct uploads from artists who grant clear platform-distribution rights.
3. Curated open-license collections after item-level review.
4. External-provider integrations that honor the provider's technical and contractual restrictions.

## Artist compensation models to explore

Upfront commissions, voluntary donations, per-pack purchases, subscription revenue sharing, or free open-license contributions. None has been selected.

## Still needed before launch

A selected software license; contributor terms; an asset metadata schema; acceptance rules for licenses; appropriate permissions for sublicensing and downloads; and a review of third-party-provider terms.
