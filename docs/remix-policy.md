# Workshop artwork: permissions and planned remix library

**Status: Original workshop consent forms reviewed on 18 October 2026. Public remix rights are NOT yet granted by those forms. No workshop PDFs should be uploaded as freely remixable material until additional permission is documented.**

## What the signed form templates actually authorize

### Artist consent form

- Artists **retain ownership** of their artwork and designs.
- The research team receives a **non-exclusive license to reuse, reproduce, and display** the materials **for noncommercial academic purposes**, including research papers, presentations, and educational displays.
- That license requires **appropriate artist credit**.
- The forms allow anonymized images in academic publications and workshop documentation, subject to their stated privacy rules.
- Artists can request withdrawal, subject to the form's limits.

### Student participant consent form

- Participant survey content and responses can be used for research analysis.
- Portions of questions or responses may be reproduced or adapted for **anonymized academic publications or conference presentations**.
- Personal information is confidential without explicit additional consent.

Neither document grants a general right to publish entire workshop PDFs on GitHub for arbitrary third-party download, copying, modification, or redistribution.

## Proposed future remix license

The project owner intends to distribute selected workshop artwork for noncommercial sharing and remixing. **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** is a candidate: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/ . It is not automatically in effect because someone signed a workshop consent form. Each relevant rights holder must grant the additional public rights, and the terms must account for creator attribution.

CC BY-NC-SA is an open Creative Commons **content license**, not an OSI-approved open-source code license. Its noncommercial condition restricts certain commercial uses; it does not itself prohibit all use of art inside software, so ask a qualified advisor if commercial platform competitors are an important concern.

## Public anonymity and attribution

The public repository should use neutral project filenames such as `P01.pdf`, `P02.pdf`. It should not include a public contributor-name directory. Names can instead be displayed in a future GUTTR artist-profile area **when creators have agreed to that attribution**. A private internal rights register may be maintained outside the public repo.

Filename pseudonyms do not remove personal details or handwritten names embedded in PDFs. Pages must be checked before publication.

## What additional permission should say

Request a separate optional permission from the relevant creators covering:

1. Public online hosting and downloads, including GitHub and GUTTR.
2. Third-party noncommercial copying, remixing, and redistribution.
3. The selected CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license and its irrevocability for copies already released.
4. Artist credit, anonymous attribution, or preferred naming and where it will be displayed.
5. Any third-party elements, participant survey material, or sensitive information included in the work.
6. Whether GUTTR itself may display/distribute the artwork if the platform later charges subscription fees, potentially through a **separate platform license**.

Consent to the original study is separate from this optional public distribution permission.

## Repository scope

Until permissions are confirmed, the GitHub repository may host general documentation, contribution guidelines, and creator-owned materials individually cleared for publication. The complete GUTTR application source remains proprietary and is not part of the public remix collection.

This page summarizes two forms supplied by the project owner: *Consent Form for Student Participants – Single-Session Survey Design Workshop* and *Consent Form for Artist Participants – Comic-Based Survey Redesign Workshop Series*. The forms are not themselves uploaded publicly.
